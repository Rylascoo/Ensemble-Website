# T3 — Perspective → Consequence Stress Test 01

Status: **NON-PRODUCTION VISUAL TRANSITION TEST / NO PRODUCT SEMANTICS**

Parent grammar: `TRANSITION_GRAMMAR_01.md`

## Question

How should one division inside a shared dark field become the material event that changes the page which follows, without reading as:
- a theme toggle;
- a slideshow wipe;
- a card replacement;
- a decorative copper accent;
- an app-state transition?

The test holds meaning constant and varies only the physical transformation mechanism.

## Shared starting state

- one dark shared field;
- three partial apertures;
- no separate-world implication;
- one selected division has the potential to acquire material weight;
- oxidized-copper warmth is causal pressure only, never generic chrome;
- resulting limestone field is a changed material condition, not “light mode.”

## Variant A — Scar Bloom

The selected divider thickens irregularly into a physical scar. Limestone propagates outward from both sides of that scar until it becomes the following field. The scar persists after the material shift.

Strengths:
- clearest causal continuity;
- outgoing structure literally becomes incoming structure;
- strongest relationship to historical Causal Scar;
- avoids card replacement;
- makes material change originate locally rather than globally.

Risk:
- if copper is too bright or smooth, it becomes a branded wipe.

Control:
keep warmth narrow, desaturated and structurally imperfect.

**RECOMMENDED INCUMBENT.**

## Variant B — Aperture Conversion

One of the three dark apertures changes to limestone first, then the neighboring apertures follow.

Strengths:
- very legible cause/order;
- simple responsive implementation.

Failure pressure:
- reads as three panels/cards changing state;
- suggests separate independent modules;
- weakens “one shared world” premise.

**REJECT AS PRIMARY. Preserve only as diagnostic contrast.**

## Variant C — Ground Rise

The selected divider reaches the bottom edge; a limestone field rises from the ground contact point and eventually fills the view.

Strengths:
- physical/material;
- connects to Stage grounding and consequence.

Failure pressure:
- can read as a curtain/wipe;
- makes vertical direction too important;
- less directly inherited from perspective division.

**SECONDARY. Do not advance ahead of A.**

## Responsive test

Desktop:
- scar may sit off-center;
- material propagation can expand horizontally.

Portrait:
- scar should remain off-center;
- material may propagate diagonally/horizontally rather than require a wide desktop spread;
- do not center the scar solely to fit the phone.

The causal origin must remain visually recoverable at all widths.

## Reduced motion

Variant A reduced-motion state is straightforward:
- dark section ends with selected divider already thickened;
- limestone section begins with the same scar entering from the preceding edge;
- no animation is required to understand inheritance.

This is a strong reason to prefer A.

## Disposition

**Advance Variant A — Scar Bloom.**

It is the only tested mechanism that preserves all three required ideas at once:
1. one shared field;
2. local event;
3. later material inheritance.

Next:
integrate Scar Bloom into the world-spine prototype as the transition between Perspective and Consequence, while leaving the other transitions abstract.
