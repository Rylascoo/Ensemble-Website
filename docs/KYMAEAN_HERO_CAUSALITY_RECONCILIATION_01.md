## 3. What the experiments actually established

### Round 01

Mechanism: environment-first rigid planar displacement / occlusion.

It successfully escaped ordinary-task historical realism and maintained restraint, but the environment became the protagonist. The changed condition and altered possibility required packet knowledge.

### Round 02

Mechanism: human-first flexible folded band / direct contact.

It successfully corrected Human Necessity, Changed-condition legibility and S1. The fold is visibly a changed state; the people are primary; the mobile semantic cluster survives.

However the band remains approximately waist-height. Restoring it to an unfolded state would still plausibly allow the same people to reach over it and make essentially the same contact. The image therefore shows:

`contact at a changed condition`

more clearly than:

`contact made possible by the changed condition`.

### Cross-round conclusion

The evidence does **not** prove that a static hero cannot satisfy the contract.

It proves that two materially different mechanisms — viewpoint/occlusion and fold/contact — failed to make altered possibility causally necessary enough to satisfy both Altered Possibility and the Removal Test.

No protected-requirement collision has yet been demonstrated. In particular:

- stronger human salience did not force changed-condition failure;
- stronger changed-condition legibility did not force Surface Escape failure;
- restraint did not force semantic failure;
- the Round 02 failure is geometric/counterfactual, not evidence that subtlety and causality are inherently incompatible.

Therefore contract revision is not yet empirically required.

## 5. Reconciliation options

### Option A — Preserve all frozen requirements and change the causal visual mechanism

**Recommended.**

Do not correct or reroll the fold/contact concept. Do not raise the band, deepen the fold, or otherwise create a near-duplicate Round 02 repair.

Change the causal architecture from **separator/access geometry** to **body-load/support geometry**.

Specific direction:

**BODY-LOAD / SUPPORT-STATE CAUSALITY**

A materially changed shared condition should visibly support, reposition, brace or otherwise carry a person's body in a way the baseline state physically could not. The altered possibility must be legible from load path, body position and material deformation rather than from a symbolic opening or viewer-reconstructed viewpoint.

The static counterfactual should be self-evident:

`restore the shared condition to baseline -> the depicted body position/use becomes physically impossible or materially collapses`.

Why this direction is stronger:

- it attacks the exact repeated root failure rather than changing style;
- body weight/support is externally visible and does not require private-state inference;
- Removal Test becomes physically testable in one still;
- it is materially different from both prior separator mechanisms;
- people can remain primary;
- it can remain sparse and non-ordinary if the changed condition is already complete rather than depicted as repair/construction;
- it does not require a portal, bridge, logo, interface, glow, explanatory graphic, or single clue prop.

Primary risks:

- could drift into furniture, platform, architecture or ordinary-task staging;
- could become a bridge/ledge shorthand if a gap dominates;
- could make the mechanism more visually important than the people;
- must preserve a sufficiently dark/compatible calm-left field for the fixed identity system.

Those risks are measurable under the unchanged gates and should be handled in a future candidate specification, not by changing the frozen contract.

### Option B — Preserve requirements but remain in separator/access geometry

Example: change the height, closure, opening or body arrangement so the baseline physically blocks contact.

**Not recommended.**

Pros:

- directly addresses the Round 02 counterfactual defect;
- operationally simple.

Cons:

- risks being a correction variant/free-reroll of Round 02 rather than a genuinely changed approach;
- keeps the project in the same divider/threshold semantic family already tested twice in different forms;
- increases risk of portal/threshold shorthand and literal explanatory staging.

### Option C — Clarify or revise the frozen semantic burden

Possible targets would be Altered Possibility or Removal Test.

**Not recommended on present evidence.**

Reason: two failures satisfy the frozen reconciliation trigger but do not demonstrate that the requirements conflict with each other or cannot be satisfied by another mechanism. Round 02's improvement on Human Necessity, changed-condition legibility and S1 is positive evidence that the contract is responsive to better mechanism choice rather than globally contradictory.

Any actual threshold or requirement change would require an explicit Director decision naming the exact text to change and why.

### Option D — Pause static hero experimentation

Preserve all requirements but stop searching for a hero artwork mechanism at this time.

Valid if the Director prefers to defer hero-art resolution while other website/product work advances.

This does not authorize motion, implementation, or a weaker hero substitute automatically.
