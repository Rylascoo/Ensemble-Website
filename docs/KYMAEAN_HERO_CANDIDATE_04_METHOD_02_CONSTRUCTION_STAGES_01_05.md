## 1. Stage 1 — Semantic Dependency Record

### Shared proposition

Two visually distinct adults inhabit one continuous visibly altered field in which **Person A's passive body load travels through one broad coupled deformation and creates Person B's otherwise unavailable elevated/inward body position; that altered B position makes one clear forearm-to-forearm human contact between A and B physically possible.** Restoring the visible baseline or removing A removes B's altered position and therefore removes the contact relation. Removing B removes the depicted altered human consequence rather than leaving a complete A-only human proposition.

No required meaning depends on intention, emotion, knowledge, memory, consent, motive, hidden authorship or prior action by either person.

### Person A observable job

- **Visible role:** passive load source in the shared condition.
- A occupies a broad low support region of the field with pelvis/lower torso and part of the upper body visibly weight-bearing.
- A's hands/arms do not manipulate, pull, hold open, brace or operate the field.
- A's body load is the visible physical input to the coupled deformation.
- **A-removal consequence:** the A-side depression/load disappears; the connected B-side uplift/advance relaxes; B's altered body position no longer exists; the direct forearm contact becomes physically unavailable.

### Person B observable job

- **Visible role:** altered-position occupant and human consequence.
- B is borne by a different continuation of the same coupled field, but B does not independently create a second copy of A's support proposition.
- B's pelvis/lower torso support is displaced upward and inward toward A by the connected changed geometry.
- B is not helping, rescuing, balancing, controlling or operating A or the field.
- **B-removal consequence:** the changed field may remain physically deformed under A's load, but the altered human consequence and A↔B contact proposition disappear. The remaining image becomes only a mechanism/load condition and therefore no longer communicates the full hero proposition.

### Shared baseline condition

A single broad human-scale field runs laterally through the center-right cluster. Outside the changed region its two long boundaries and one secondary structural contour remain approximately parallel, low-relief and mutually aligned on the same simple baseline plane.

The baseline is not a separate object or prior frame. It is recoverable from the still because unchanged portions on both sides of the changed region visibly return to the same simpler alignment.

### Visibly changed condition

Across the center-right, the same field departs from its simple aligned baseline in one broad distributed **coupled flexure**:

- an A-side support region is displaced downward under A's passive body load;
- the continuous field then sweeps through a broad oblique transition rather than a hinge/pivot;
- a B-side support region is displaced upward and inward;
- both outer boundaries and the secondary structural contour remain visibly continuous through the deformation and rejoin the simpler baseline organization beyond it.

The deformation is one shared field state, not two independent supports, a seesaw, a rigid beam, a device, a chair pair, a bridge, a portal or a separate sculptural object.

### Altered present possibility

B can occupy a stable elevated/inward body position close enough for one neutral forearm-to-forearm contact with A.

Under baseline restoration, B's support anchor returns lower and farther outward; the same body position cannot remain and the forearm contact lies outside the available reach geometry.

### Directed dependency

`visibly changed continuous coupled field + A passive body load -> B support anchor displaced upward/inward -> B occupies otherwise unavailable body position -> direct A↔B forearm contact becomes physically possible`

### Human-primary intention

The visual event is the **two-person relation produced by the shared changed condition**. The field is causal infrastructure. If the people are mentally removed, the remaining flexure is incomplete evidence rather than the primary proposition.

## 7. Pre-registered risks for Stage 6 / later rendering

The construction does not claim these are solved until later geometry/pixels prove them:

1. **seesaw/device drift:** the distributed flexure may render as a mechanical lever;
2. **sculptural-furniture drift:** the field may read as purpose-built seating/installation rather than changed state;
3. **generic-occupant drift:** renderer may make people interchangeable despite asymmetric geometry;
4. **B alternate-support drift:** renderer may add floor/ledge/secondary body support;
5. **helper/rescue drift:** contact may be dramatized into support, saving, operating or intentional assistance;
6. **field-protagonist drift:** the coupled shape may gain too much optical weight and fail S1;
7. **baseline-loss drift:** renderer may smooth away one correspondence family, especially in mobile;
8. **hidden-coupling drift:** the returned pixels may not make A-to-B load transfer visually plausible even if scaffold geometry encodes it;
9. **contact-convenience drift:** renderer may change body/arm geometry so contact remains possible under baseline restoration;
10. **responsive drift:** figure scale or changed geometry may expand outside the fixed mobile semantic envelope.

Any of these is a candidate failure or a pre-render construction revision, not grounds for post-hoc scoring changes.
