A younger seated person and an older seated person face one another across a central shared surface and physically engage around a small object or cup. ## 2. Formal classification

> **VID-01 V0R1 — DIAGNOSTIC / TRANSFERABLE LAW ONLY — HUMAN RELATIONAL FIELD STRONG / CAUSAL RECOVERABILITY PARTIAL-FAIL / SURFACE UNSELECTED.**

The image is artistically coherent and human-first, and it demonstrates that a relationship-led visual field can carry tension, asymmetry, specificity, and history-like atmosphere without interface or explanatory text.

The artwork does not make a particular earlier human choice recoverable enough from the present organization, nor does it make a materially altered present possibility legible enough to distinguish consequence from an emotionally charged conversation.

Therefore the surface does not survive as a current visual-family candidate. Its useful findings survive as research evidence.

## 3. Evaluation rubric

### A. Artistic integrity — STRONG PASS

The image stands as complete artwork without labels, UI, branding, explanatory border, or product framing.

It has coherent painterly handling, atmosphere, spatial depth, human specificity, and a readable relational center.

The work does not look like a software mockup or causal diagram.

### B. Human primacy — STRONG PASS

People are the dominant carriers of meaning.

The four figures are visually distinct and occupy different relational positions rather than acting as interchangeable scale figures.

The composition makes relationship consequential to how the scene is read: two people form the central exchange, one person stands apart under visible tension, and another watches from the near foreground.

### C. Causal recoverability — PARTIAL FAIL

The image suggests that something precedes the present encounter, but suggestion is not sufficient causal evidence.

Visible papers/photographs and the charged arrangement plausibly indicate prior history. They do not establish:

- what earlier human choice occurred;
- who made it;
- how that choice transformed the present condition;
- what current affordance, access, relationship, constraint, or possibility is materially different because of it.

A viewer can invent many compatible stories. That openness is artistically valid, but the causal law under test requires more present-state evidence than this render supplies.

Promoted finding:

> **History-like atmosphere is not the same as causal recoverability.**

### D. Consequence / changed possibility — PARTIAL FAIL

The scene contains relational tension and a current exchange, but the visible organization does not clearly demonstrate a changed present possibility caused by a prior choice.

The image might depict a disclosure, confrontation, repair, refusal, decision, or ordinary difficult conversation. The artwork itself does not determine which.

Promoted finding:

> **A charged relationship does not by itself prove that prior action changed what someone can do now.**

### E. Epistemic restraint — PASS WITH CAUTION

The people occupy visibly different spatial and participatory positions. This supports asymmetry without text.

However, facial expression, folded arms, gaze direction, and physical distance must not be converted into claims about Knowledge, Belief, Memory, guilt, intent, or who understands the earlier cause.

The image succeeds when read as unequal participation/position. It fails if the Director invents private mental-state authority from pose.

### F. Character identity / specificity — PASS FOR SINGLE-IMAGE SPECIFICITY; TRANSFORMATION UNTESTED

Each person is visually specific and non-interchangeable in this image.

The experiment does not test identity persistence across crop, transformation, alternate representation, scale change, or later medium change. No Character representation system can be selected from this result.
