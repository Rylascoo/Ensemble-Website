## Research question

> **Does the same temporal information gain survive when the later benefit comes from changed organization within an ordinary shared arrangement rather than from gaining physical access?**

## Visual reading

The same four people and shared tabletop appear at two directly comparable moments.

Earlier moment:

- one man is visibly reorganizing shared tabletop elements;
- notebooks/tools are being repositioned;
- the other people remain self-directed.

Later moment:

- the originating actor is seated and no longer acting on the arrangement;
- the changed placement of shared materials persists;
- another person independently uses the newly available central work area for a different activity.

The causal route is organizational rather than physical-access based.

## Principal findings

> **Minimal temporal comparison materially improves recoverability of authorship, persistence and later enabled use.**

TEO-02 additionally establishes:

> **That temporal information gain survives removal of the literal physical-access route used by TEO-01.**

The later capability is still relatively ordinary and local: reorganization clears/repositions shared materials so a different person can use the changed workspace differently. This is sufficient to demonstrate a different semantic route. It does not prove deep distributed systemic necessity.

## Why the temporal branch stops here

The remaining weakness is degree, not category.

TEO-01 proved temporal information gain with an access shortcut. TEO-02 proved that the same earlier/later comparison remains useful when the benefit is organizational rather than access-based.

Further attempts to make the later capability more philosophically complete would re-enter the overconstraint pattern already demonstrated by PAC:

- contraption/product/installation risk;
- explanatory storyboard pressure;
- narrative over-seeding;
- increasingly low information value.

Therefore:

> **Do not design TEO-03.**

## Transferable law
