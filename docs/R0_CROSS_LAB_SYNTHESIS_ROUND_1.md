## Transferable laws

### Human causality becomes changed possibility

Prior action is strongest when it changes what later people can do, know, access, inherit, resist, maintain, misunderstand, repair, exploit, or further alter.

### Consequence changes affordance or constraint

Meaning is stronger when action changes possibility, access, constraint, relationship, composition, hierarchy, or state rather than merely adding a symbolic trace.

### Shared reality can remain asymmetrical

Several people can inhabit one authoritative reality while having unequal access, knowledge, interpretation, desire, benefit, or responsibility.

### People are causal participants

People should not function as scale figures or decorative observers. Presence, choice, refusal, maintenance, withholding, crossing, repair, or further action should materially matter to the depicted state.

### Relationship is visual structure

Distance, orientation, overlap, negative space, interruption, occlusion, recurrence, gesture, possession, and attention can carry relational information.

### Identity can survive transformed representation

Recurring identity can persist across changes of crop, scale, orientation, pose, viewpoint, completeness, medium, and context when a coherent family of perceptual invariants survives.

### Environment can carry causal history without looking old

History does not require ruins, patina, wear, antiquity, magical traces, or flashbacks. A present condition can prove prior action through changed organization, exception, correspondence, access, or affordance.

### Changed relationship outranks literal object

A bridge, opening, wall, door, repair, path, or threshold is only a scene solution. The transferable principle is that human action changed how conditions relate and what became possible afterward.

## Late crossing / boundary branch

The late synthesis produced a useful research shorthand:

> **We inherit boundaries. We create crossings.**

This phrase is **not** a public tagline, not the governing Kymaean thesis, not a frozen visual law, and not authorization for a new phase.

The preserved principle is narrower:

> **Human choices can alter inherited conditions and create new relationships or possibilities.**

### R0 Synthesis 06

Reinforced inherited architecture.

Transferable value: intervention became visible as an environmental fact; a small human alteration could change the meaning or affordance of an inherited condition.

Failure risk: restoration / architectural archaeology; humans remained too observational.

### R0 Synthesis 07

Opened monumental boundary.

Transferable value: human intent visibly changed a massive inherited condition and therefore changed possibility.

Failure risk: dystopian / post-collapse wall imagery and over-explicit causality.

### R0 Synthesis 08

Active bridge/crossing through inherited boundary.

Transferable value: the changed condition was being used rather than merely observed; the strongest idea was changed relationship / changed possibility.

Failure risk: literal `wall = separation` / `bridge = connection` symbolism.
