## 1. Purpose

V0R7 demonstrates that independent action, anti-single-carrier composition, local action-response and whole-surface formal divergence can all exist without establishing a distributed H5 performance field.

The remaining ambiguity is propagation.

ARI-01 asks:

> **Can visible action-response propagate through at least three independent people so that changing an upstream action forces downstream compositional reorganization, without relying on one shared object, common intention or private-state claim?**

This remains H5 `Expressive Performance Field` research. It does not select a visual family.

## 2. Governing distinction

V0R7 promotes:

```text
local action-responsive composition
!=
distributed action-field interdependence

pairwise action-response
!=
distributed action relay
```

A successful ARI probe should make at least three actions part of one visible dependency chain.

Minimum abstract structure:

```text
Action A
-> materially changes the usable/visual situation of B
-> B visibly adapts through a different action
-> B's adaptation materially changes the usable/visual situation of C
-> C acts inside that changed field
```

The actions need not be cooperative, harmonious, simultaneous in intent, or equally understood.

## 3. Downstream action-substitution test

ARI-01 strengthens AFI's substitution test.

For an upstream action A:

> **Keep A's person in approximately the same region and scale but replace A with a materially different action. Would B's visible action and at least one downstream C-region need to reorganize?**

For B:

> **Replace B's response while preserving approximate placement. Would C's available action/field need to reorganize?**

If the answer is no, the image may still be a collection of local action islands.

## 4. Interlock requirements

A valid probe should satisfy all of the following:

1. at least three distinct people participate in the central visible dependency chain;
2. each person remains independently specific and non-interchangeable;
3. A's action changes B's available space, route, reach, balance, opening, interruption, or timing;
4. B visibly responds through a materially different action rather than merely posing near A;
5. B's response changes C's available space, route, reach, balance, opening, interruption, or timing;
6. C visibly acts inside the changed situation;
7. no single large shared object, barrier, sheet, mechanism or scenic element carries the chain;
8. the image does not resolve into three separate vignettes;
9. no shared intention, cooperation, hierarchy, motive, knowledge or emotional interpretation is required;
10. the relation remains legible from visible action and spatial organization alone.

## 5. What ARI is not

ARI is not:

- a choreography requirement;
- synchronized group action;
- a story beat requiring inferred motive;
- a Rube Goldberg mechanism;
- a diagram of cause and effect;
- a turn-taking sequence;
- a timeline;
- an instruction to depict collision or danger;
- a requirement for one common prop;
- a selected Kymaean visual language.

The desired property is **distributed present-tense action consequence**, not narrative explanation.

## 6. Formal-control strategy

V0R7A already demonstrates at probe level that positive depiction-level formal control can produce strong whole-surface divergence. V0R7B demonstrates a weaker but useful contour/void departure.

ARI-01 therefore does **not** need another two-way formal micro-batch immediately.

The next experiment should isolate the unresolved semantic variable:

> **Can the action-response chain itself become structurally necessary?**

The renderer may choose medium, palette, setting and degree of abstraction freely. Any resulting surface remains quarantined.

This avoids granting V0R7A incumbency merely because its formal probe was strongest.

## 7. Falsification conditions

ARI fails if:

1. only two people visibly affect one another while the rest remain independent;
2. A, B and C are merely close together but their actions do not force downstream change;
3. replacing A's action leaves B and C essentially unchanged;
4. replacing B's response leaves C essentially unchanged;
5. one large object or barrier carries the entire dependency;
6. the actions synchronize into one collective gesture;
7. the image depends on inferred motive, emotion or knowledge to read the relation;
8. the renderer produces three separate local action vignettes;
9. the chain is explained by arrows, labels, text, panels or timeline structure;
10. a visually novel surface is treated as carrier success without passing the downstream substitution test;
11. a carrier pass is treated as visual-family convergence.
