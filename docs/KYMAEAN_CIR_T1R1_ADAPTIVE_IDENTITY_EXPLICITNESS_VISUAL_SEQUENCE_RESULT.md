## 0. Purpose

Test the CIR-T1/CIR-T1V1 temporal identity contract visually across four controlled moments:

1. First Encounter;
2. Established;
3. Focus;
4. Reduced / image-limited / narrow explicitness.

The render is research evidence only. It does not select the photoreal office, cast, typography, labels, leader lines, Opportunity styling, Watch icon, board annotations, or any final Kymaean app/website surface.

## 1. Formal judgment

> **CIR-T1R1 — ADAPTIVE EXPLICITNESS SEQUENCE PASS / PRODUCT-SURFACE PARTIAL FAIL**

The sequence demonstrates the central CIR-T1 behavior:

```text
first encounter
-> explicit identity

established low ambiguity
-> local labels recede

focus / task need
-> selected identity becomes explicit again

reduced visual certainty
-> broader semantic identity returns
```

This validates reversible identity presentation without restoring a permanent portrait roster.

## 2. Strong passes

### 2.1 First-use discoverability

Riven, Wren and Vale are directly identifiable in the first-encounter state without face recognition, color coding, A/B/C translation, or a duplicate roster.

### 2.2 Established-state recession

The established state removes local name labels while preserving the living relational field. `Perspective: Wren`, `Current Opportunity: Vale`, and Watch remain independent references.

This demonstrates:

> **A local Character label may recede without forcing all state references to recede.**

### 2.3 Selective re-explicitness

The focus state restores `Riven` without relabeling every Character.

This visually supports:

> **Identity explicitness may be locally selective while Character identity remains globally stable.**

### 2.4 Reduced visual certainty

The reduced/image-limited/narrow condition restores direct naming for all present Characters, supporting the CIR law:

> **As visual certainty decreases, semantic identity explicitness increases.**

### 2.5 Identity remains identity

Unlike CIR-R1, no personality, profession, psychology, archetype, intent, Knowledge, Observation or biography copy is appended merely to make Characters memorable.

This is a strong visual confirmation that:

> **Identity disclosure is not Character summarization.**

## 3. Material surface failures / quarantine

### 3.1 Opportunity still overweights research alias `C`

The render visibly presents a large `C` token beside human-readable `Vale`.

This only partially satisfies CIR-T1. Product-facing authority should resolve directly to `Vale`; research alias `C` has no product-facing inheritance rights.

### 3.2 Eye icon for Watch remains semantically risky

The eye glyph may read as observing/seeing and therefore risks collision with Kymaean's fictional Observation semantics. Patch authority explicitly prevents visual/co-present state from becoming Observation authority.

The eye treatment remains quarantined.

### 3.3 Floating labels / dots / leader lines remain annotation-like

The association works semantically, but the treatment resembles image annotation, tracking, AR/computer-vision labeling, or state markers. CIR identity recovery requires no biometric recognition or continuous machine identification.

Direct name association passes; this geometry does not gain inheritance rights.

### 3.4 Static board cannot prove recognition timing

The four states are visible simultaneously to the evaluator. A real creator experiences them sequentially. Therefore the render cannot prove when an implementation has earned the right to recede a label.

CIR-T1's anti-timer law remains binding:

> **Elapsed time alone does not prove recognition or justify recession while ambiguity remains.**

### 3.5 Board annotations are research-only

`First Encounter`, `Established`, `Focus`, `Reduced / Accessible`, their explanatory subtitles, `PEOPLE DRIVE POSSIBILITY`, and `A QUIETER INTERFACE. A DEEPER STORY.` are presentation-board annotations, not candidate active-Stage copy.

Active Stage product-thesis/marketing narration remains prohibited by default.

### 3.6 Accessibility terminology correction

The fourth panel should not imply accessibility is a special visual mode. The actual design meaning is reduced/image-limited/narrow explicitness. Accessibility must be present in every state; real keyboard/screen-reader/WinUI accessibility remains implementation-stage validation.

## 4. Promoted findings

> **Identity can become quieter without becoming weaker.**

> **A local Character label may recede while human-readable state references remain explicit.**

> **Adaptive identity explicitness can be visually bidirectional: explicit -> quiet -> selectively explicit -> quiet again, with stronger explicitness whenever visual certainty falls.**

These extend, rather than replace:

> **Labels may recede; recoverability may not.**

## 6. Stop decision

CIR-T1 has sufficient architectural and visual evidence at the identity-behavior level. Further iterations on the same office/control surface are unlikely to provide proportional new information.

The next major design problem is no longer identity mechanics. It is:

> **What visual identity and visual world could belong specifically to Kymaean while preserving the now-mature app/website semantic laws?**

CIR-T1R1 therefore closes the current identity-mechanics visual laboratory and hands forward only its validated laws, not its surface.
