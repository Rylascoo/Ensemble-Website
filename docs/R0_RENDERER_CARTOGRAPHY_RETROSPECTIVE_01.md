## Most important retrospective correction

The first four Cross-Surface experiments made the renderer appear inescapably attracted to historical architecture, bridges, thresholds, and period-coded realism because their formal grammar was left open.

The earlier AL laboratory contradicts that pessimistic interpretation.

When the Director explicitly constrained formal behavior, the renderer produced meaningfully different non-architectural grammars:

- `AL-B01`: flat graphic human field using simplified bodies, massing and negative space;
- `AL-C01`: shallow layered human/material field using occlusion, seams and gaps;
- `AL-D01`: sparse line, incomplete contour, gesture and void;
- `AL-E01`: translucent overlap in which interaction altered shared visual conditions without merging identities;
- `R1-STUDY-C`: reduced surface detail that strengthened human/relational reading;
- `V04-ORDINARY-PRESENCE`: human-scale ordinary reality with local non-spectacular visual change.

Therefore the accumulated evidence supports the methodological correction:

> **The renderer is capable of substantial surface divergence when formal grammar is explicitly controlled. Leaving formal grammar open is itself a strong experimental variable and repeatedly invites high-probability genre shortcuts.**

This finding is stronger than the hypothesis that account memory alone explains the repeated architecture/bridge results.

It does not prove account-level context is absent; it makes that hidden mechanism less useful as the primary experimental explanation because the same product surface has already produced orthogonal outputs under different formal constraints.

## High-confidence useful control mechanisms

These are not visual identities. They are experimental mechanisms that successfully changed renderer behavior.

### Formal flatness + negative space

Evidence: `AL-B01`.

Effect: people remained structurally important without relying on portrait realism or deep scenic architecture.

### Sparse contour + active void

Evidence: `AL-D01`; identity caution from `AL-G01`; identity solution evidence from `AL-H01`.

Effect: relationship can be carried by gesture, distance and mark density; persistent distinguishing features are needed to avoid identity-attribution failure.

### Shallow overlap / occlusion

Evidence: `AL-C01`, `AL-E01`.

Effect: social asymmetry and influence can be encoded through visibility, overlap, shared regions and interrupted edges rather than literal walls/bridges.

### Reduced descriptive detail

Evidence: `R1-STUDY-C`.

Effect: human and relational reading strengthens while environmental/object symbolism loses dominance.

### Human-scale ordinary reality

Evidence: V0.4 Ordinary-Room Presence Study.

Effect: human-caused visual change can remain meaningful without monumental spectacle or overt magic.

### Repeated-pattern exception

Evidence: `RI-010`, `RI-011`.

Effect: present state can robustly reveal prior organization and changed consequence.

This remains a causal mechanism, not an artistic grammar.

## Controlled Formal Matrix 01 — proposed

The first controlled matrix deliberately reuses the **relational semantic route that failed in CSR-E04**, because that provides a useful falsifiable baseline.

Fixed semantic kernel:

> Three distinct people share one present situation. One person's visible choice changes the relationship among them in the depicted moment. The others respond differently because their understanding is unequal. People must carry the structure and meaning.

This deliberately avoids changing the semantic hypothesis again. The experimental change is formal control.

### CFM-01A — Flat Mass / Negative Space

Formal package:

- shallow picture plane;
- large contiguous human shapes;
- deliberate negative space;
- minimal depth cues;
- proximity, overlap, separation and void carry relationship;
- surroundings subordinate and visually simple.

Evidence basis: `AL-B01`.

### CFM-01B — Sparse Contour / Void

Formal package:

- sparse, incomplete contours;
- high unfilled-space proportion;
- small set of persistent distinguishing features per person;
- gesture, orientation, distance and mark density carry relationship;
- descriptive scenery withheld.

Evidence basis: `AL-D01`, with identity safeguards from `AL-G01` and `AL-H01`.

### CFM-01C — Shallow Overlap / Occlusion

Formal package:

- compact human forms in shallow depth;
- controlled overlap and occlusion;
- relation changes edges, visibility and shared regions;
- each person remains separately traceable;
- environmental depth/background description remains minimal.

Evidence basis: `AL-C01`, `AL-E01`.

## Controlled variables across all three

Hold constant:

- semantic kernel;
- three distinct people;
- one visible present choice;
- unequal understanding/response;
- artwork-only contract;
- people dominate the composition;
- restrained non-theatrical illumination;
- no brand/product/UI/presentation framing;
- no reference images in Matrix 01.

Do not add historical-surface blacklists to the renderer packet. The formal grammar itself is the control.

## Render budget and stop rule

Maximum: **3 renders**, one fresh regular non-project Renderer Context each.

Execution order: CFM-01A -> CFM-01B -> CFM-01C.

Stop early before CFM-01C if both first two outputs collapse into the same quarantined architectural/period/cinematic attractor family despite formal control.

Stop after the third render regardless of apparent success. Archive and synthesize before any further generation.

If CFM-01B loses individual attribution, record that as a formal-grammar failure rather than entering a corrective prompt chain.

## What Matrix 01 can and cannot prove

It can test whether the same relational semantic kernel survives materially different controlled formal grammars and whether E04's bridge/monumental result was substantially caused by leaving formal language open.

It cannot establish final Kymaean aesthetics, full causal-history robustness, motion language, wordmark design, palette, website composition, or identity-through-transformation by itself.

A later matrix can reintroduce causal history/changed possibility after the formal-control method is validated.
