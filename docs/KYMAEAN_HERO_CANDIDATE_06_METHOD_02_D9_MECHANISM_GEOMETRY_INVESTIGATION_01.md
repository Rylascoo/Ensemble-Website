## Candidate family H1 — residual shear-slip support patch + side/back passive brace

### Structural proposition

One broad continuous finite-thickness field has a simple low-relief baseline organization visible on both sides of the human cluster. Multiple structural correspondences — outer boundary, thickness return and one longitudinal registration contour — establish that baseline.

Inside the cluster, one local **residual shear-slip zone** displaces a connected support patch **inward toward A and slightly tilts it laterally**. The patch remains part of the same field; it is not a detached platform, shelf or object.

Person A occupies a stable baseline-adjacent region with an independently stable body axis.

Person B's feet/lower support remain fully field-borne on the displaced tilted patch. The changed support point moves B inward toward A and tilts B's depicted body axis just enough that B's center-of-mass projection lies outside B's available foot/support polygon toward A by a positive margin.

B is stabilized by one broad passive upper-back/shoulder-to-A-upper-torso contact. A uses no hands and does not carry B vertically.

### Baseline restoration

Restoring the shear-slip zone to the simple field alignment:

- translates B's support patch outward;
- removes the lateral tilt;
- makes B's depicted baseline posture independently stable;
- moves B's shoulder/back contact locus away from A by a positive separation margin.

The brace relation therefore disappears geometrically rather than merely becoming optional.

### Human arrangement control

Preferred construction orientation:

- A and B are side-by-side or slightly opposed in body orientation, not face-to-face;
- A's hands/arms remain non-intervening;
- B's contact is upper-back/shoulder or rear/lateral torso, not chest-to-chest or hand contact;
- neither gaze nor expression is required;
- B is not depicted mid-fall, reaching for help or recovering from an accident.

This is a geometry/semantic anti-attractor control, not final pose styling.

### Strengths

- removes A-as-field-author burden entirely;
- changed state can be carried by residual registration mismatch rather than hidden force;
- B's altered possibility has an explicit support/COM model;
- A's physical necessity is direct and human-scale;
- vertical load remains field-borne, reducing rescue shorthand;
- baseline restoration can combine both rotation and translation, making the relation disappear by positive margin;
- compact geometry fits the existing mobile semantic envelope;
- the people, not the field, carry the final stabilizing event.

### Risks

- shear-slip patch may look like a deliberately designed ramp/platform;
- displaced field could read as earthquake/damage/hazard shorthand;
- body brace may read as dance, exercise or helping;
- B lean may become too dramatic/cinematic;
- a visible registration contour may become diagram-like or decorative;
- A may read as a static wall-like support rather than a specific person if body geometry is generic.

### Safeguards

- no crack/rubble/debris/damage spectacle;
- no platform edge, detached slab, stair, rail, frame or architectural support system;
- shear displacement is local and modest, with both baseline witnesses visible;
- multiple correspondences recover baseline without one dramatic scar;
- B's lean is small enough to remain quiet but large enough to satisfy the positive stability margin;
- A remains visibly specific and independently balanced;
- no active arm gesture from either person;
- contact carries lateral/rotational reaction only;
- sanitized transfer geometry must fail to read as a complete designed ramp/platform when B is removed; otherwise reject before renderer.
