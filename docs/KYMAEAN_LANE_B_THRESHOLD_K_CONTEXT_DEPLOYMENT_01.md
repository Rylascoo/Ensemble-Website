# KYMAEAN LANE B — THRESHOLD K CONTEXT DEPLOYMENT 01

Status: STATIC CONTEXT DEPLOYMENT PASS / HISTORICAL THRESHOLD K RETAINED / WORDMARK ROLE NARROWED  
Date: 2026-09-05  
Workstream: SHARED BRAND / I0 BEFORE W0

## Purpose

Test the recovered historical Threshold K unchanged in four realistic brand roles before constructing a bespoke KYMÆAN wordmark.

The mark geometry is unchanged; source-board artwork is isolated only for placement.

Every visible `KYMÆAN` treatment is a neutral measurement proxy, **not a wordmark proposal**.

## Governing law

**Do not redesign the mark to solve a layout problem.**

If a context requires regularizing Threshold K, the deployment fails.

## 01 — Native app titlebar

Design judgment:
- 18 px is very quiet and the negative K is less immediate;
- 24 px gives the strongest current balance between recognition and application scope;
- 31 px begins to make routine app chrome feel brand-led rather than Production-led.

Result:
**Threshold K can function in titlebar scale without becoming the interface.**

24 px is a study reference, not a production metric.

## 02 — App icon / standalone tile

Design judgment:
- generous inset under-uses the tile;
- medium inset preserves both icon presence and internal negative-space discovery;
- tight inset lets the outer tile visually compress the mark and makes it feel more like a conventional glyph.

Result:
**The icon container should frame Threshold K, not participate in constructing it.**

No app-icon padding, tile geometry, color or packaging is selected.

## 03 — Website header lockup

Design judgment:
- tight coupling makes Threshold K feel like a decorative initial;
- moderate separation lets mark and name retain distinct jobs while reading as one identity;
- an oversized emblem-led lockup overweights the symbol for routine navigation.

Result:
**Threshold K works best as an independent lead element rather than a badge or letter substitute.**

## 04 — Website hero

Design judgment:
- large horizontal parity creates unnecessary competition;
- mark-as-prelude gives the clearest separation of jobs;
- name-first also works and proves the symbol does not need to lead every branded surface.

Result:
**The mark/name hierarchy can adapt by context while identity remains stable.**

## Cross-context result

The recovered historical Threshold K needs **breathing room** more than geometric correction.

Across the study it is strongest when:
- its internal negative space remains self-contained;
- surrounding frames do not visually complete the K;
- the mark remains distinct from the name;
- branding does not displace application/Production scope.

No modified Threshold K is justified by this round.

## Wordmark consequence

The previous idea of making the `Æ` visibly echo Threshold K is no longer the strongest next move.

The future bespoke `KYMÆAN` should:
- remain unmistakably authored;
- have a deliberately resolved `Æ`;
- carry strong spacing and proportion;
- stand alone when the mark is absent;
- use **lower emblematic density** than Threshold K;
- avoid containing, repeating or completing the logo.

The next wordmark question is:

**How can the name carry equal ownership with quieter means?**

This narrows W0 without selecting typography or final letter geometry.
