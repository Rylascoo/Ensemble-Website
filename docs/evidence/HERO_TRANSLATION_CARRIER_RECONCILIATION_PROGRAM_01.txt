MATERIAL-DIFFERENCE RULE

A carrier hypothesis is materially different only if it changes the depiction logic by which the viewer can recover the prior simpler condition and perceive its causal relation to present human possibility.

Changing palette, material name, setting, texture, camera, atmosphere, historical genre, or surface styling alone does not create a new carrier hypothesis.

A materially different carrier must differ in at least two of these representation-level dimensions:
1. where baseline/change correspondence is encoded;
2. how the changed state remains identifiable as the same shared condition rather than a different object/place;
3. how B's present posture is visibly constrained by that change;
4. how restoration of the simpler baseline would force the A↔B relation or altered possibility to change;
5. whether causality is distributed across the composition or concentrated in one literal object.

PROMPT-SEED DISCIPLINE

HTCR-01 reasons first in depiction logic, not concrete renderer nouns. Material names, architectural nouns, terrain nouns, devices, portals, bridges, walls, ruins, platforms, damage, cracks, scars, diagrams and explanatory markers are not allowed to stand in for causal structure.

A surviving mechanism must remain intelligible when concrete surface/material/setting nouns are removed. This protects against renderer-prior convergence and against mistaking one familiar prop for the formal relation.

HTCR-A1 TEST

A1 must generate materially different depiction/carrier logics and test each against all of the following before promotion:

A1.1 — intrinsic baseline recoverability: the simpler prior condition can be inferred from present-state organization without text, before/after comparison, damage shorthand or private history;
A1.2 — changed-state identity continuity: the viewer can read current and recoverable prior states as the same shared condition, not two unrelated objects/places;
A1.3 — posture causality: the changed condition visibly constrains B's present support/posture rather than merely accompanying it;
A1.4 — restoration counterfactual: restoring the simpler baseline would force a material change in B's support/posture and therefore in the A↔B relation or altered possibility;
A1.5 — human necessity: both specific people remain necessary and non-interchangeable;
A1.6 — no single-prop literalization: the formal control is not satisfied by one dominant physical prop carrying the entire idea;
A1.7 — shorthand resistance: no architecture-as-already-designed, terrain, damage/accident, device/platform, diagram or transfer-medium explanation becomes the materially simpler reading;
A1.8 — Surface Escape plausibility: removing the impossible/transformed condition would not leave an ordinary task/accident genre tableau as the primary reading;
A1.9 — identity coexistence: the left identity/copy region can remain compositionally calm without becoming a pale fixed-light patch that behaves as the artwork's independent illumination source;
A1.10 — responsive survival: the causal relation can plausibly remain legible in the frozen mobile crop without relying on peripheral explanation.
