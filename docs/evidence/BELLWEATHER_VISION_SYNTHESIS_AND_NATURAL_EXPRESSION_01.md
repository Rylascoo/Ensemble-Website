## Findings
The synthesis board confirms the Human / Persistent / Iconic Bellweather framework while showing that identity survives scale, circumstance, weather, private/public context and emotional variation.
The natural-expression family confirms that Bellweather remains recognizable with reduced accessory dependence and more lived-in presentation. The after-party study strengthens time-persistence by showing the same formal look after hours of use; the full-body study remains an exploratory historical-specificity branch rather than replacement gown authority.

The Director identified broad visible-teeth smiles as less natural for Bellweather. The target expression language therefore favors closed-mouth, slightly asymmetrical, or subtly parted-lip expressions, with emotion carried primarily through eyes, brows, gaze, posture and fine mouth tension. This is a Bellweather direction constraint, not universal human-expression law.

The six-expression study is useful exploration evidence but is not deterministic facial-geometry authority because minor panel-to-panel face drift remains.

## Successor boundary
Next exploration should focus on front-facing and near-front identity stress testing while holding facial construction tighter than before: direct frontal portrait, shoulders-square upper body, seated frontal, standing frontal, and restrained emotional variations with 10–20 degree head turns.
