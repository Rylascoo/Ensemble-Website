# R0 Temporal Observability Experiment 01

Status: **DIRECTOR DESIGN CHECKPOINT — NO RENDER AUTHORIZED**

Updated: 2026-09-03

## Purpose

This experiment follows `docs/R0_CROSS_MEDIUM_SEMANTIC_ALLOCATION_AUDIT_01.md`.

The bounded research question is:

> **Does adding minimal temporal evidence materially improve recoverability of prior human authorship and enabled possibility without forcing literal mechanism, hierarchy or narrative explanation?**

This is not a motion-system proposal, website-animation design, hero specification, or phase transition. It is one observability test.

## Why one two-moment artwork

A separate static baseline render is unnecessary because the experiment can contain its own control.

One generated artwork will show the same people and same scene at two directly comparable moments. Evaluation will score:

1. what each moment communicates independently;
2. what becomes recoverable only when the two moments are compared.

This makes **temporal information gain** measurable without opening an A/B render branch.

## Controlled variable

The only new semantic resource is:

> **direct comparison between an earlier moment and a later moment**

The experiment does not introduce a selected carrier family, named setting, culture, occupation, technology, genre, architecture, medium or historical period.

## Required semantic structure

The artwork must contain two adjacent moments of the same scene.

Across both moments:

- the same distinct people remain recognizable;
- viewpoint, scale and surrounding world remain substantially constant;
- people remain structurally primary;
- any shared arrangement remains ordinary, modest and visually subordinate;
- no one becomes a helper, rescuer, instructor, leader or controller.

Earlier moment:

- one person is visibly completing a small change to an ordinary shared arrangement;
- the other people remain self-directed;
- the change is attributable to that person through visible action rather than explanation.

Later moment:

- the earlier change remains after the originating action has ended;
- the original actor is no longer controlling or guiding the condition;
- a different person independently performs an action whose fit with the later condition is materially clearer than it would have been in the earlier condition.

The pair should allow a viewer to recover:

> **who changed the condition -> what persisted -> how a later independent action became possible or materially better supported**

without captions or explanatory text.

## Exact sterile renderer packet — NO RENDER AUTHORIZED

> Create one artwork showing the same small group of distinct people at two adjacent moments, with the same viewpoint, scale, and surrounding world in both moments.
>
> In the first moment, one person is visibly completing a small change to an ordinary shared arrangement. The other people remain present and self-directed.
>
> In the second moment, a short time later, that change remains even though the person who made it is no longer acting on it. A different person is independently doing something that makes clear use of the changed condition. The contrast between the two moments should make the causal sequence recoverable: who changed the condition, what persisted, and how the later action depends on that changed condition.
>
> Keep the same people recognizable across both moments. No one should guide, rescue, instruct, direct, or control another person. Keep the changed arrangement modest, ordinary, and visually subordinate to the people.
>
> Use a shallow, restrained composition with minimal environmental spectacle and enough repeated spatial structure that the two moments can be compared directly. Keep the visual treatment consistent across both moments.
>
> Return only the artwork. Do not add text, captions, arrows, labels, diagrams, interface elements, promotional framing, or surrounding presentation layout.

## Evaluation gates

### TEO1 — Cross-moment correspondence

Are the people, viewpoint, scene and relevant arrangement clearly the same across both moments?

### TEO2 — Visible authorship

Does the earlier moment clearly attribute the change to one specific person through visible action rather than inference from role or expression?

### TEO3 — Persistence

Does the changed condition clearly remain after the originating action has ended?

### TEO4 — Later independent use

Does a different person remain self-directed while making materially meaningful use of the persistent changed condition?

### TEO5 — Enabled-possibility gain

Does comparison with the earlier moment make it visibly clearer that the later action is enabled or materially better supported by the changed condition?

### TEO6 — Temporal information gain

Does the two-moment comparison add causal information that neither moment alone reliably provides?

A pass requires the pair itself to improve recoverability. If the result is understandable only because one panel contains an already-literal bridge, doorway, repair mechanism, helper scene or other self-explanatory symbol, temporal evidence has not demonstrated the intended gain.

### TEO7 — Independent agency

Do all people remain self-directed without helper/recipient, leader/follower or controller/controlled hierarchy?

### TEO8 — Human primacy

Do the people carry most of the artwork's structure, action and meaning?

### TEO9 — Ordinary / non-symbolic condition

Does the changed condition remain mundane and subordinate rather than becoming a contraption, product, installation, special technology or symbolic device?

### TEO10 — Attractor and narrative suppression

Does the result avoid collapsing into bridge/path/door/threshold/access/repair/monumental-progress shorthand, cinematic problem-solution storytelling, storyboard exposition or infographic explanation?

## Success threshold

A meaningful positive result requires passes on TEO1 through TEO6 plus preservation of TEO7 through TEO10.

The core measurement is not whether a two-panel image looks understandable. It is whether **the temporal comparison itself** materially improves authorship and enabled-possibility recoverability while the known failure attractors remain suppressed.

## Failure interpretations

### If authorship improves but enabled possibility does not

Temporal evidence helps recover origin but does not solve the capability problem.

### If enabled possibility improves only through literal mechanism

Temporal evidence remains confounded with the same access/repair/threshold shortcuts seen earlier.

### If the sequence becomes helper/rescuer narrative

The test fails independent-agency preservation.

### If the result becomes a storyboard or infographic

The test demonstrates explanatory sequencing, not a viable visual-semantic mechanism.

### If correspondence fails

No conclusion about temporal information gain is valid because the two moments cannot be compared reliably.

## Recursive design audit

### Variable isolation

PASS.

The experiment adds direct earlier/later comparison while keeping the semantic target already established by SOV/RST/PAC: human authorship, persistence, independent agency and enabled possibility.

### Need for an additional static baseline

REJECTED.

TEO6 provides an internal comparison: score the two moments separately, then score the pair. A second generation would add cost and surface variance without being necessary for the first temporal question.

### Need for literal carrier examples

REJECTED.

Examples are known visual seeds. The packet permits an ordinary shared arrangement but does not name furniture, architecture, tools, technology or setting.

### Formal-control sufficiency

PASS WITH LIMIT.

The packet fixes repeated viewpoint/scale, shallow composition, restrained surroundings and consistent treatment. It does not select a medium or palette. This is enough to support comparison while preserving renderer latitude.

### Identity correspondence

NECESSARY CONTROL, NOT A NEW PRODUCT CLAIM.

Recognizable recurring people are required only so the viewer can compare the same scene across moments. Existing AL evidence already supports identity-through-invariants as visually feasible.

### Storyboard risk

MATERIAL BUT ACCEPTABLE FOR ONE DIAGNOSTIC.

Two moments inherently introduce sequence. The packet suppresses captions, arrows, labels, interface framing and overt presentation. If the renderer still produces explanatory storyboard grammar, record that as failure rather than correcting it.

### Literal affordance risk

MATERIAL.

The phrase `how the later action depends on that changed condition` may still activate access/help/repair shortcuts. Removing the dependency requirement would invalidate the measurement. The risk is therefore retained and scored rather than hidden with a blacklist.

### Motion claim

NONE.

This is a two-moment temporal-evidence test rendered as one comparative artwork. A positive result would justify further temporal research; it would not prove that animation, interaction or website motion is selected or necessary.
