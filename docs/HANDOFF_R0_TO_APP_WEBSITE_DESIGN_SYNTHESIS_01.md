# 6. Validated R0 laws the design phase may inherit

These are **constraints and semantic resources**, not aesthetic selections.

## 6.1 Human primacy

Distinct people can remain structurally and semantically important across very different formal grammars.

Design implication:

> When people appear, do not use them merely as anonymous decoration, lifestyle filler, or scale figures.

This does not require photography, illustration, crowds, portraits, or any specific human-representation style.

## 6.2 Relationship as visual structure

Distance, orientation, overlap, negative space, interruption, occlusion, recurrence, attention, grouping, and material relation can carry meaning.

Design implication:

> Layout and state relationships can communicate structure; not every relationship needs a label, line, or metaphor.

## 6.3 Identity through coherent invariants

Recurring identity can survive changes in crop, scale, pose, orientation, completeness, and viewpoint when enough perceptual invariants persist.

Design implication:

> Identity continuity need not depend on one permanent portrait/icon representation.

Do not turn this into a required character-art system unless product use earns it.

## 6.4 Shared reality can be asymmetrically available

Static artwork strongly supports **asymmetric evidentiary position**, not proof of private mental state.

Design implication:

> Different views may legitimately reveal different evidence from one authoritative underlying state.

Do not visually claim to know a person's private belief, intention, memory, or interpretation unless the product actually has that authority.

## 6.5 Present structure can carry history

Pattern/exception, interrupted correspondence, and altered organization can make prior state/history recoverable.

Design implication:

> History does not require faux age, ruin, patina, nostalgia, or explicit flashback.

## 6.6 Visible authorship and independent agency are in tension

Direct physical causality improves actor attribution but tends to collapse into helper/controller/rescuer/conflict semantics.

Design implication:

> Do not purchase causal clarity by making one person merely control another.

## 6.7 Independent agency is not enabled agency

A person acting independently does not prove that an earlier condition enabled the action.

Design implication:

> If the product/website claims changed possibility, the interface or sequence must supply actual evidence of the change.

## 6.8 Object presence is not systemic causality

PAC established:

- supported action is not necessarily carrier-dependent action;
- object affordance is not configuration affordance;
- local relational affordance is not shared systemic affordance;
- shared-element overlap is not distributed configuration dependence;
- distributed carrier topology is not distributed configuration dependence.

Design implication:

> Do not assume dense UI, shared objects, diagrams, or visible infrastructure automatically communicate system-level causality.

## 6.9 Static observability has a boundary

Strong still-image evidence exists for:

- human specificity/primacy;
- relational geometry;
- transformed-but-recognizable identity when deliberately tested;
- asymmetric evidentiary position;
- independent agency;
- present consequence;
- some recoverable history when the image contains its own evidence;
- ordinary persistent affordance.

Still imagery did **not** robustly prove every case of:

- specific prior authorship;
- definite prior human creation/configuration;
- full `prior choice -> persistent condition -> newly enabled action` chain;
- distributed no-single-element common cause;
- private mental state.

Transferable law:

> **Not every system law must be directly observable in one static visual surface.**

## 6.10 Temporal comparison is a validated causal-observability resource

TEO-01 and TEO-02 establish:

> **Minimal earlier/later comparison materially improves recoverability of specific human authorship, persistence, and changed possibility.**

TEO-02 further establishes that this gain survives removal of TEO-01's literal physical-access route.

Design implication:

> When a product or website surface needs to communicate what changed because something happened earlier, state transition or before/after evidence is empirically justified as a candidate technique.

This does **not** select animation, split-panel composition, scroll choreography, a hero treatment, or any particular motion system.

## 13.7 Accessibility and inclusive interaction

Treat accessibility as design architecture, not final polish.

- keyboard-first use;

## 13.8 ARM64 / battery / native-feasibility review

For every major app visual or interaction proposal, ask:

- Does this create idle work?
- Does it imply continuous GPU animation?
- Does it require background inference?
- Can the state be event-driven?
- Can visual continuity be achieved cheaply?
- Is NPU work actually necessary and capability-gated?
- Does it remain native WinUI rather than pushing toward an embedded web-app aesthetic?

## 13.9 Website performance / implementation-feasibility review

For every major web proposal, ask:

- Can this be semantic/static first?
- What JS is actually necessary?
- Is the effect worth its runtime cost?
- Can SVG/CSS replace canvas/WebGL?
- Does it survive reduced motion?
- Does it preserve Core Web Vitals and low-end-device usability?
