# KYMAEAN — ATTRIBUTABLE AFTERMATH GATE 01

Status: FROZEN BEFORE PROMPT / FROZEN BEFORE RENDER  
Date frozen: 2026-09-05

## Purpose

R1 showed:

`COMPLETED ACT -> PERSISTENT AFTERMATH -> CHANGED POSSIBILITY`

while failing:

`WHICH PERSON CAUSED IT`

This gate tests that missing property.

## Hypothesis

**A still can preserve recoverable earlier human authorship when a completed act leaves a persistent condition whose present structure corresponds specifically to its originating person and cannot be explained equally well by later contact, coincidence, or anomaly.**

Attribution is stronger than proximity, contamination, prominence, expression, or color matching.

No prompt may exist before this gate is frozen. No mandatory criterion may change after the image exists. New issues may be recorded only as non-gating observations.

## Mandatory binary gates

**All must PASS. No compensating average.**

### A1 — Completed act
The originating act ended before the depicted moment. The source may inspect/react/coexist, but may not be visibly performing the action that created the aftermath.

### A2 — Persistent aftermath
The changed condition remains materially/spatially present without ongoing source action.

### A3 — Specific source
The frame must support **this depicted person caused this earlier**, not merely "someone caused it."

### A4 — Residue-embedded correspondence
The aftermath itself contains a source-specific physical correspondence embedded in the field/material, not only on the person's body.

Possible evidence classes: bodily scale, prior-route geometry, clothing/material continuity, characteristic deformation, repeated mark, orientation/compression pattern.

### A5 — Independent second cue
A second, nonredundant present cue points to the same source. It may not be merely the same color/material, proximity, gaze, or expression.

### A6 — Later-contact confound
The evidence cannot be explained equally well by the supposed source merely encountering/crossing the aftermath after someone or something else created it.

If "it stuck to them later" remains equally plausible, FAIL.

### A7 — Rival-source exclusion
The affected person, natural process, accident, or unexplained anomaly must not remain an equally plausible source.

### A8 — Altered present possibility
The aftermath materially changes what another depicted person can occupy, reach, place, use, avoid, perceive, or do now. Mood alone fails.

### A9 — Source-person necessity
Removing the source person would materially weaken authorship recoverability.

### A10 — Affected-person necessity
Removing the affected person would materially weaken the changed-possibility reading.

### A11 — Aftermath removal
Removing the aftermath destroys both elapsed-time information and changed present possibility.

### A12 — Distributed evidence
No portable prop, mechanism, doorway, portal, path, tool, container, or obvious clue object carries most attribution burden.

### A13 — One world / epistemic restraint
One authoritative shared world only. Use physical trace, availability, occlusion, and position; do not assert private knowledge, belief, memory, guilt, or intent.

### A14 — Temporal residue
The frame reads primarily as **aftermath**. Present-tense maintenance, cleaning, breaking, pouring, moving, constructing, repairing, or activation of the causal change fails.

### A15 — Desktop identity zone
The left ~40% is genuinely expendable/quiet and contains no indispensable person or attribution evidence.

### A16 — 4:5 causal cluster
One plausible 4:5 crop retains source person, affected person, aftermath, both attribution cues, and altered possibility.

## Automatic attribution failures

FAIL if any is equally plausible:

1. **Contact-only:** source merely touched/crossed residue later.
2. **Natural anomaly:** nonhuman process could have produced it just as plausibly.
3. **Prominence:** source is inferred only because closest/largest/centered.
4. **Expression:** guilt/surprise/pride carries authorship.
5. **Color match:** shared color carries authorship.
6. **Prop clue:** one object tells the story while aftermath is secondary.
