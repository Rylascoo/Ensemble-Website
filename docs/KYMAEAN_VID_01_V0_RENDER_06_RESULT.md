### Presence-field vs action-field distinction — MATERIAL PARTIAL FAIL

The crucial counterfactual is not only `remove the person`.

Ask instead:

> **If the same person occupied the same approximate body region but performed a different action, would the field materially reorganize?**

For most figures, probably not enough.

Examples:

- the seated figure could be tying a bag rather than adjusting footwear and would still supply essentially the same lower-left mass;
- the central person could carry a different object or merely stand while preserving much of the central compression;
- the upper-right person could face the wall or adjust another garment while preserving the same dominant rust/white shape;
- the crouching figure could reach toward another small object while preserving the same lower-right weight.

Therefore the artwork demonstrates **distributed people-dependent composition**, but not yet a field whose current organization is strongly caused by several specific human actions.

Promoted law:

> **A field can depend on several people as compositional presences without yet depending on what those people are doing.**

Related distinction:

```text
distributed figure-ground dependency
!=
distributed action-field causality
```

## 5. Method conclusion

DFI-01 is validated as a useful correction, but V0R6 reveals the next level of ambiguity.

The next method question is not `how do we distribute people across the composition?`

It is:

> **How can a field depend on what several people are doing, rather than merely on where their bodies are placed?**

That requires an action-substitution test in addition to the person-removal test.

Promoted method law:

> **For performance-field testing, replacing a relevant action while preserving approximate body placement should materially change the field; otherwise the field may be presence-dependent rather than action-dependent.**
